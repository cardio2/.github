<h3 align="center">AI that explains. Rules that protect.</h3>

Cardio2 builds an AI companion that helps people recover at home after a heart attack, a stent, heart surgery or a heart failure diagnosis. It turns hospital discharge papers into a simple daily plan, guides a 12-week home rehabilitation programme and keeps the cardiologist informed between visits.

Cardiac rehabilitation saves lives, yet most patients in Europe never receive it. Cardio2 is built for the months after discharge, for patients, their families, doctors and nurses, hospitals and insurers.

> **Status:** in development. Not yet certified as a medical device and not yet for use in treatment.
> In an emergency, call **112**.

## What the AI does

- Reads discharge letters and turns a photo or PDF into a clear list of diagnoses, medicines and restrictions, which a nurse then confirms.
- Explains medical guidelines in short, calm answers in the patient's own language, always with a source.
- Writes a one-page report for the cardiologist, where every figure can be traced back to the original measurement.
- Adapts walking and exercise within the limits set by the doctor, and supports healthy habits (planned).

## What the AI does not do

- It never starts, stops or changes a medicine or a dose.
- It never judges danger signs. Chest pain, sudden breathlessness or fast weight gain in heart failure are handled by fixed medical rules that send the person to 112 or to their medical team.
- It never shows an answer without a source that can be checked.
- It does not diagnose and does not replace the cardiologist.

## Where we are

Today: concept proven, prototype in progress (technology readiness level 3-4).

- **Months 0-3 (current stage):** clinical advisory board, safety rules, prototype, quality system started
- Months 3-9: first version of the app and a first clinical study in 2-3 centres
- Months 9-18: certification documents prepared and submitted, first contracts with hospitals
- Months 18-24: CE certification, heart failure module, applications for insurance coverage
- Months 24-36: covered by insurers in the first countries, 15+ centres, early-warning model
